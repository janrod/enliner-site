Collaboration

Share it without
giving it away.

Enliner can go multiplayer, one vault at a time. Two people edit the same outline live, edits made offline merge cleanly when we reconnect, and the vault stays plain Markdown the whole time.

Real-time, offline-first

A movable-tree CRDT (Loro) merges everyone's changes with no central authority deciding who wins. Work on a plane, reconnect, and it reconciles on its own.

A relay we can run

Sync moves through a small relay written in Rust. It forwards sealed bytes and never sees our text. Host it ourselves on hardware we control, down to a Raspberry Pi on our own network.

End-to-end encrypted

Everything on the wire is sealed to keys only the members hold. Even the relay that carries our vault around cannot read a word of it.

The idea behind it

Code got cheap.

Code got cheap

For forty years, the expensive part of software was the code. It needed skilled engineers, months of their time, and a lot of money before there was anything to sell. That is why software companies look the way they do. They raised the money, hired the engineers, and had to earn it back.

That cost is falling fast. I built Enliner mostly by describing it to AI agents and supervising the work. It is a real native Mac app: tens of thousands of lines of Swift, a custom text engine, our notes stored as plain Markdown on disk. It opens a ten-thousand-block vault in under a second and answers a keystroke in under nine milliseconds. It carries more than seven thousand automated tests. In the old currency of engineering hours, it cost almost nothing.

I am putting this first because it is the whole point. When code gets cheap, one person can build serious tools. And once one person can build them, we stop having to accept the tools we are handed.

The deal we are being offered

Most AI software asks for the same thing. Put our notes, our calls, our thinking on their servers, and they will make it smart for us. It sounds fair. We get a good product, and we never have to run anything ourselves.

Read the rest of the deal. Our thinking lives on their machines, under their retention policy. It trains their next model. It becomes an asset on their balance sheet, and we pay every month to reach it. The day we leave, we leave it behind.

That trade made sense when running the software was genuinely expensive. Someone had to pay for the servers and the engineers, and the subscription covered them. Those costs are now falling out from under the deal. The compute can run on the Mac we already own, and the code is cheap to write. What is left is a monthly fee to use our own thinking, held on hardware we do not control.

For a business the cost runs past the invoice. It is the liability of our conversations sitting in someone else's system. It is the options we give up when one vendor holds our data and moving it somewhere else is hard.

Use the tool, don't become the tool

I am not against AI. I use it every day, and Enliner is built on it. AI is a genuinely good tool, and we should use it.

The question is who it serves. Once a model can read everything we give it and repeat it back, the thinking we hand over stops being only ours. So use AI the way we use any good tool. Point it at the work, keep the results, and stay in charge of the process. Hold on to our judgment, and keep the things we know close.

There is something fitting about building independence with the very technology being used to erode it. The tools Big Tech is selling are the same tools that let a small shop walk away from Big Tech. That is the move Enliner makes.

Independence is a design choice

Independence does not mean running our own servers. Almost no one wants that, and I am not asking anyone to.

It means choosing where we depend on someone else, on purpose, and keeping the door open. Our knowledge lives in plain files we can read without the app. The parts that run in the cloud stay swappable, so we can carry them elsewhere. We lean on the cloud where it earns its place, and we keep the rest on our own machine. Dependence we pick deliberately, and can reverse, is fine. Dependence that arrives as the default, with no way out, is the one that costs us.

This is a design problem as much as a political one. Every one of these choices is a decision someone makes for us, inside the software. Enliner makes them in our favor, and it makes them in the open.

What Enliner is

Enliner keeps our notes, calls, and voice memos on our own Mac and turns them into something we can think in: pages, links, and a graph that connect what we know. The AI runs on our own machine, on local models. Nothing is uploaded, and nothing trains a model. We can confirm all of it by turning on airplane mode and watching everything keep working.

We buy it once. Our knowledge stays in plain Markdown we can open in ten years, with or without Enliner. Stop paying, and it keeps running. That is what owning a tool used to feel like, and it still can.

The honest part

Enliner is a working prototype, built in the open. The app and its AI features run and are measured on real hardware. The parts that turn it into something we can buy, the signing and the store and the last of the polish, are not finished yet.

On-device models are smaller than the frontier models in the cloud. For summarizing our own calls and notes they are good, and they are ours. For the hardest tasks the cloud is still ahead. I am betting that keeping our thinking on our own machine is worth more than the last increment of quality. That is a bet, and I would rather say so than pretend it away.

I am also building this at a deliberate size: a focused tool from a small shop, made to do a few things well. That is the point, and it is worth saying plainly so no one expects the other thing.

Own our thinking

Code got cheap, and that hands us a choice most of us never had. We can keep renting our thinking back from the companies that store it, or we can build tools that keep it ours and use AI on our own terms.

Enliner is my answer, written as software so the stance is more than words. Use AI as a tool. Keep what we know. Own our thinking.
