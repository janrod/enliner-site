Design principles

Principles, built
into the software.

Anyone can write a values page. These are the design decisions Enliner actually runs on, the kind you can verify by using it. Every one is made in your favor, and made in the open.

  1. 01

    Personal
    compute

    The transcription, the search, and the AI all run on the Mac in front of you, on models that live on the machine. Computation stays where you are, instead of on rented silicon you reach across the network. That is the whole point: the value of the work stays with the person doing it.

  2. 02

    Offline
    first

    Enliner needs no login and no server to do its job, and everything works offline at full strength. You can prove it in five seconds: turn on airplane mode, and it all keeps working.

  3. 03

    Your data
    stays plain files

    Your vault is standard Markdown on disk, one file per page, and those files are the original your work lives in. The app keeps a database for speed, but it is only an index: delete the app and your writing is still there, readable in ten years by anything that opens text.

  4. 04

    Sync that
    can't read you

    Sharing a vault is optional. When you turn it on, sync moves through a small relay that only ever sees sealed bytes, with everything end-to-end encrypted to keys the members hold. If you would rather trust no one, you can run the relay yourself, down to a Raspberry Pi on your own network.

  5. 05

    Buy it once,
    keep it for good

    You pay a single price and the app is yours, with no subscription metering your own notes back to you. Stop paying for updates and nothing is taken away: the version you have keeps working, offline, for as long as you want it.

Why it's built this way

The argument
behind the principles.

These commitments come out of a bigger question about where computing should happen as the cloud moves to meter all of it. That is the philosophy behind the project.
